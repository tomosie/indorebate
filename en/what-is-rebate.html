What Is a Rebate?

A rebate is a share of commission paid to traders, where the amount is determined by the IB. The illustration starts with a trader who wants to trade forex. To access the forex market, the trader must open an account with a broker. When the trader registers through an Introducing Broker (IB), the broker pays the IB a commission as a reward for bringing in a new client. Some IBs then share part of that commission with the trader as a token of appreciation. This sharing of commission is called a rebate. In terms of trading rules, traders who register through an IB and those who register directly with the broker get the same trading conditions. The only difference is that traders who join through an IB receive cashback on every trade they make.

How Does It Work?

👤 Trader Opens an Account
🏦 Broker Pays Commission to the IB
🤝 IB Shares Part of the Commission
💰 Trader Receives a Rebate

Rebates on Profit or Loss

Profitable Trades

When a trade is profitable, the trader still earns a rebate according to the broker's terms.

Losing Trades

Even when a trade ends in a loss, the rebate is still paid as long as the broker's conditions are met.

This is what makes rebates so popular with traders. Although the rebate per trade looks small, if you trade regularly the accumulation can become very large. However, every broker has different rules for calculating rebates, for example based on lots, volume, or certain conditions. So traders still need to understand each broker's terms.
